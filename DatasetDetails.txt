Dataset Sources
===============

HeQ (Hebrew Question Answering Benchmark)
------------------------------------------
Authors:   Keydar et al.
Venue:     Findings of EMNLP 2023
Size:      30,147 QA pairs (train 27,142 / val 1,501 / test 1,504)
Format:    Extractive reading comprehension (SQuAD-style span extraction)
Sources:   Hebrew Wikipedia and Geektime (Israeli tech news)
Niqud status: NONE. Both source corpora are modern Israeli prose written in
            standard unvocalized Hebrew script, as is the overwhelming norm in
            contemporary written Hebrew. The data is niqud-free as distributed.
Annotation: Crowdsourced under a controlled protocol designed to address
            morphological ambiguity in Hebrew; includes revised EM/F1 metrics
            that account for non-transparent span boundaries in morphologically
            rich forms.
HuggingFace: pig4431/HeQ_v1
Paper:     https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01812

ParaShoot
----------
Authors:   Keren & Levy (Tel Aviv University)
Venue:     Proceedings of the 3rd Workshop on Machine Reading for Question
           Answering (MRQA @ EMNLP 2021)
Size:      ~3,000 crowdsource-annotated QA pairs (train / dev / test splits)
Format:    Extractive reading comprehension (SQuAD-style)
Sources:   Hebrew Wikipedia
Niqud status: NONE. Hebrew Wikipedia articles are authored in unvocalized
            modern Hebrew; no diacritics appear in the passages or questions
            as distributed by the original authors.
Annotation: Human-annotated following SQuAD crowdsourcing methodology;
            the first native Hebrew QA dataset, designed to support few-shot
            and cross-lingual transfer research.
Repository: https://github.com/omrikeren/ParaShoot
Paper:     https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11314

MKQA — Multilingual Knowledge Questions & Answers (Hebrew subset)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Authors:   Longpre et al. (Apple Research)
Venue:     Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics (TACL), 2021
Size:      10,000 QA pairs aligned across 26 languages; Hebrew subset retains
           ~7,000 examples after filtering unanswerable and null-answer instances.
Format:    Open-domain QA (no supporting context); answers are short entities,
           values, or phrases.
Sources:   Questions sampled from Natural Questions (Kwiatkowski et al., 2019)
           and human-translated into Hebrew by professional annotators; answers
           are represented in a language-independent canonical form.
Niqud status: NONE. Questions were translated by professional annotators
            into standard unvocalized modern Hebrew. Answers are predominantly
            named entities, numerals, and short fixed phrases — categories that
            are inherently niqud-invariant.
Role in this study: Serves as a negative control. Because answers are largely
           proper nouns, numerals, and fixed entities, diacritization is expected
           to have minimal impact, providing a contrastive baseline against the
           reading-comprehension tasks above.
Repository: https://github.com/apple/ml-mkqa
Paper:     https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.15207

Usage in This Study (Hebrew)
----------------------------
All three datasets are originally distributed without niqud, reflecting the
natural state of modern written Hebrew. This means the no-niqud condition
(baseline) is the data as-is, and no stripping is required beyond a
precautionary Unicode pass to catch any stray diacritic characters. The
with-niqud condition is produced by running the baseline text through an
automatic diacritization tool (dicta-il/dictabert-large-char-menaked, i.e.
Nakdan/DICTA) on the context and question fields only; answer strings are
held fixed across both conditions. The primary metric is the delta in
token-level F1 and Exact Match (EM). HeQ and ParaShoot are the primary
benchmarks; MKQA serves as a negative control where the niqud effect is
hypothesized to be near zero.


ARCD (Arabic Reading Comprehension Dataset)
--------------------------------------------
Authors:   Mozannar, Maamary, El Hajal, Hajj
Venue:     Proceedings of the Fourth Arabic Natural Language Processing
           Workshop (WANLP @ ACL 2019), Florence, Italy
Size:      1,395 QA pairs (evaluation set used in this study)
Format:    Extractive reading comprehension (SQuAD-style span extraction)
Sources:   Arabic Wikipedia articles
Tashkeel status: NONE. Arabic Wikipedia prose is written in standard
            undiacritized Modern Standard Arabic, as is the overwhelming
            norm in contemporary written Arabic.
Paper:     "Neural Arabic Question Answering"

TyDiQA (Arabic subset)
------------------------
Authors:   Clark, Choi, Collins, Garrette, Kwiatkowski, Nikolaev, Palomaki
Venue:     Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics
           (TACL), 2020
Size:      15,726 QA pairs (evaluation set used in this study)
Format:    Extractive/information-seeking QA over Wikipedia passages,
           typologically diverse multilingual benchmark
Sources:   Arabic Wikipedia
Tashkeel status: NONE, undiacritized as distributed.
Paper:     "TyDi QA: A Benchmark for Information-Seeking Question Answering
           in Typologically Diverse Languages"

MKQA — Multilingual Knowledge Questions & Answers (Arabic subset)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Authors:   Longpre, Lu, Daiber (Apple Research)
Venue:     Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics
           (TACL), 2021
Size:      4,966 QA pairs (evaluation set used in this study)
Format:    Open-domain QA (no supporting context); short entity/value answers
Sources:   Questions sampled from Natural Questions, human-translated into
           Arabic by professional annotators; answers in a
           language-independent canonical form.
Tashkeel status: NONE, undiacritized as distributed.
Repository: https://github.com/apple/ml-mkqa

Usage in This Study (Arabic)
------------------------------
All three Arabic datasets are originally distributed without Tashkeel. The
plain condition is the data as-is; the Tashkeel condition is produced by
running the plain text through Farasa (Abdelali, Darwish, Durrani, Mubarak,
"Farasa: A Fast and Furious Segmenter for Arabic", NAACL 2016 Demonstrations)
on the context and question fields only, with answer strings held fixed
across both conditions. A causal ablation ("postfix") re-encodes the
identical Tashkeel diacritics at a different Unicode/tokenizer-boundary
position (see data/arabic/postfix_tashkeel/ and results/arabic/postfix_silma/)
to isolate tokenization placement from diacritic presence.
